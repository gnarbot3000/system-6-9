Public summary

System 6.9

Community DIY endless shuttle powered by battery or hard-wired. Open software. Big-box builds. Size the motor and pack for your use case.

Mission

System 6.9 is a community project: open-source software plus builds from materials you can get online or at Home Depot, Harbor Freight, Lowe’s, Menards, and similar. The center of gravity is community and community development — not a finished commercial tow product.

Each builder has their own use case. Motor size and battery pack (or hard-wired supply) are chosen by that use case — we do not ship a one-size powertrain.

One-liner

DIY endless shuttle for the community — one architecture, many surfaces. Water (foil/skim), backyard snow, sled uphill, skate tow, and more. You size the motor and pack for your use. We publish the guide, the software and real build costs, all free. Use at your own risk.

Why build one

Commercial endless tows such as System 2.0 cost tens of thousands of dollars. System 6.9 is for hobbyists who want to compare ideas, understand what a build needs and what it really costs, and then make one for their own lake, pond or hill. Compare community builds or read the free guide.

Prototype build

Phase 0 — Design freeze Done

One-motor endless loop. Drive A-frame = power + control. Return A-frame = idler + tension. Soft ramp, E-stop, low tow height (~0.8–1.5 m).

Phase 1 — Bench prove Now

Keyed hub → capstan grips multi-wrap under a hard dead pull; E-stop kills power. Do not go to the lake until this passes.

Phase 2 — Empty lake shuttle

Full line (~80–150 m span), two A-frames, springs + straps, anchors/guys. Pass = clean reverse cycles with no rider.

Phase 3 — First rides

Carrier + handle. Foil first (~12–18 km/h), then skim (~25–35 km/h) with spotter + E-stop ready.

Parts list

Approximate USD from research spot-checks — will move; use at your own risk. Not a certified tow system. Span ~80–150 m → endless loop ~200–350 m of line. Pick battery or hard-wired — don’t double-count.

Capstan rule: do not surge bare AmSteel-Blue on aluminum. Use jacketed UHMWPE, polyester drive wraps, or a grooved keyed drum.

Powertrain — pick one path

Path What’s in it Approx USD
Battery lean ~1.5–2 kW BLDC + ESC, 48 V LiFePO4 ~40–50 Ah, light reduction $560–1,100
Battery comfortable / adult deep-water skim High-kW / 80100-class path + high-C pack + proper reduction $1,500–4,000+
Hard-wired lean 2–3 HP VFD + 1.5–2 HP keyed 3φ + mount/coupling $450–850
Hard-wired adult deep-water 5–10 HP motor + matching VFD $600–1,500+

Lean is cruise / youth / assisted-plane. Adult ~100 kg deep-water skim needs ~8–12+ kW peak / ≥~200 kgf — size for the start, not cruise. Youth ~30 kg can stay lean–comfortable with torque gearing + a long soft ramp.

Buy order

  1. Pick power path (battery vs hard-wired) and use-case size (youth vs adult deep-water).
  2. Buy line (longest lead / largest $).
  3. Capstan + return sheaves — prove grip before load.
  4. Two short towers + guys/anchors.
  5. Tension + take-up.
  6. Electrical safety panel.
  7. Dry run at 20–40 m before full 80–150 m.
  8. Empty shuttle → foil → skim.
